  \begin{abstract}
{This study presents a cartographic framework for exploring geophysical setting of Panama using the Generic Mapping Tools (GMT). Besides the economic importance as a maritime transport hub, the region of Panama is notable for vulnerable environment and high biodiversity. At the same time, seismic activity in this region is high due to the specific geologic setting, including tectonic plate subduction. To investigate the geophysical setting in this region through advanced mapping, we propose a GMT-based solutions for spatial data processing, which extends the classic GIS methods to the programming approach. First, gravity, topographic, and geodetic data were processed and imported into the GMT. Second, the seismicity dataset was collected from the IRIS open source (coverage 1970-2021). Third, the series of compatible maps was generated using console scripts written in GMT language. Afterwards, the variations in geophysical parameters were explored and compared on the maps to highlight the correlations between the processes. To help users reuse the presented scripts we presented the scripts in open repository for repeatability in similar projects regarding the seismicity and hazard risk assessment.}

\section{Introduction}
This article introduces a series of new maps of Panama prepared using scripting practical approach of Generic Mapping Tools (GMT). The aim is to visualise high-resolution geophysical, topographic and seismic data for analysis of their correlations. The main technical advantage of scripts consists in the repeatability. Thus, such maps can be easily fabricated for other regions of Latin America using GMT and similar datasets, and then assembled through a sequence of GMT codes that regulate the appearance of maps through modules and operations. Compared to previous approaches of using GMT \citep{McNamara,Kobayashi,Cleveland} we presented the scripts in full availability for the reuse in future studies. As some of the recent papers have shown \citep{4814952,Lewis,Zhangetal2017,Eakin} GMT is an excellent instrument for semi-automatic mapping of the geophysical variables.

\subsection{Background}
Novel cartographic technologies have been rapidly developed over the last decades to support spatial data analysis in marine and Earth sciences. Mapping exploits a wide variety of of theoretical principles and practical techniques to create real-world tangible reproductions of Earth models. A special application of mapping techniques including cartographic scripts used for geographical data processing of Panama. 

As a response to the needs for high-quality mapping of the regions in seismic risk, such as Central America, the presented approach provides an open source solution of GMT-based standardised cartographic techniques employed for mapping seismicity, topography and geophysical grids. Although the proposed scripting approach requires, to some extent, advanced skills in geospatial programming, GMT can be considered as an alternative method to GIS in terms of mapping and cartographic visualization. Thus, this approach could be beneficial in spatial analysis of geophysical setting over Central American region, such as seismicity and hazard risk assessment, where visualization of high-resolution spatial data is a key part of research.

From the point of view of geospatial applications, using GMT provides a novel formalism in spatial data processing to design a series of maps with identical parameters for comparability and compatibility. This coding formalism provides enough degree of freedom to map geophysical and seismic maps for hazard analysis in Panama -- a key transport region of Central America. Consequently, it enables to efficiently model 2D and 3D datasets that characterises the topography of the Earth. The systematic reuse of scripts has been applied from modular techniques of GMT and adjusted to the particular datasets within the framework of this project. Additionally, this study applies standard GMT coding, to achieve unification of maps through the development of scripts, integration of data and implementation of GMT modules using its standard syntax in a cartographic practice.

\subsection{Motivation}
Probably the most well-know tools for cartographic visualization and spatial analysis is Geographic Information System (GIS) \citep{Longley,Harvey}. While initially the range of GIS was very limited with restricted functionality, modern technologies enable a wide range of techniques to be used. Currently, besides creating maps, GIS enable to manipulate, organise, and display spatial information \citep{LAMONT1997469}. The cartographic tasks include terrain analysis, topology, generalisation, spatial analysis, data formatting, projecting, gridding, spatial clipping, overlay, logical queries,  extracting data, raster to vector conversion, map design, and many more. 

At the same time, the algorithms of mapping have been improved substantially along with the increase and precision of available open spatial data. Consequently, mapping accuracy in terms of reproduction has reached high cartographic standards. Nevertheless, using commercial GIS is perceived as being too expensive for the standard users, while open source GIS may have restricted functionality. Moreover, the input data have to satisfy certain geospatial characteristics (data formats, resolution, coverage and compatibility), in order to produce reliable high-quality maps representing the models of the geospatial phenomena and processes on the Earth.

Decades ago, principally new paradigms for cartographic data processing and mapping were proposed through scripting \citep{WesselSmith1998,Neteler2004,Neteler2008}. The main idea of using scripts in cartography was to drastically automate, and thus, to simplify, the overall mapping procedure by fabricating programming scripts for plotting maps, which would enable repeatability and precision of mapping. This class of methods os principally different from GIS since it relies on a simple concept: every step in a mapping process is fully controlled by the lines of code, written according to the syntax of embedded language. In such a way, hidden obstacles and bugs, often in traditional GIS, are overcome and avoided, and the complete procedure of mapping is radically improved.

\subsection{Contribution}
In this study, the GMT methods were applied and redesigned for mapping Panama in order to demonstrate the efficient digital series of maps on geophysical, topographic and seismic parameters of the territory of the country. Thus, this study proposes an updated strategy of using scripts to plot illustrative visualisations of topographic and geophysical setting of the country using high-resolution recent data. Furthermore, this paper extends the classical GIS workflow of mapping by integrating programming methods of data processing in geographical studies. In addition, the designed of map in the presented series was formulated using GMT scripts, to achieve the high map printing quality in a novel, structurally sound perspective of cartographic workflow. We have demonstrated how GMT syntax can be exploited to efficiently represent complex geophysical models of the terrain. 

Thus, using GMT, we proposed an efficient strategy of mapping to visualise several geophysical variables using different datasets in a united series of maps. In addition, the GMT mapping method also takes into account both 2D and 3D data visualization, which has been demonstrated in this article. the presented scripting method can be used for analysis of correlations between the data for showing various continuous fields on compatible maps to take advantage of data comparison for a better perception of links and association among the geophysical and topographic parameters. In such a way, this articles presents an automated procedure of mapping to ensure that quality of maps.

\section{Related Works}
Accurate cartographic visualization has been a long-standing challenge in marine science and oceanic engineering, particularly when the data and materials vary according to different data sources, resolution, and spatial extent. The wide range of geographic datasets poses a great challenge for mapping approaches due to their different functionality: desktop GIS, remote sensing GIS, server GIS and so on \citep{STEINIGER2013136}. Therefore, selecting suitable GIS program is important prior to mapping, since the functionality of program may affect the spatial analysis and limit cartographic techniques. A complete review of various GIS is beyond the scope of this article. We refer the read to the for a comprehensive survey on available free GIS \citep{LEIDIG201549,CHEN2010253}, recent trends in the development of cartography \citep{WangFu} and new directions and perspectives in GIS \citep{Lietal2022,DUBE2022661}, while this section only covers the most relevant GIS applications for environmental mapping.

Many existing algorithms of data processing are available only in certain programs and software. For example, some well-know commercial Geographic Information System (GIS) are focused on bitmap or LiDAR data processing. The efficient solutions are presented by ERDAS Imagine with multiple applications of image processing of Panama \citep{Guzman,SANCHEZAZOFEIFA20092076,PORTILLOQUINTERO2010144}
The Global Mapper GIS, includes a variety of tools including LiDAR handling, 3D rendering, watershed delineation. It is developed by the USGS U.S. survey, a provider of geological and environmental data, used in studies of Panama \citep{WALKER2021105632,HOPP2016187,ODEA201259}. 

Probably the most-well known and widely distributed commercial GIS is the ArcGIS which perform well both for vector and raster data including LiDAR \citep{HIGGINS2014358}, Landsat TM images processing \citep{SESNIE20082145}, deforestation analysis of mangroves ecosystems \citep{LOPEZANGARITA201882,MEJIARENTERIA2018233}, analysis of traffic in the Panama Canal and conservation \citep{GUZMAN2020104113,DARIEN2021105490,ROMPRE2008129} and hydrological basin analysis, due to the extensive spatial modelling tools available in the ArcGIS \citep{SALGADO2020138444,PEREZCONSUEGRA2021107590}. The Geomedia GIS software, developed by the Hexagon Geospatial, was a concurrent for the ArcGIS for a long period, mostly used for cadastral mapping, landscape and terrain analysis and spatial modelling for geomorphometry tasks \citep{KLINGSEISEN2008109}. The analysis of tropical environment, marine reserves and coral reef can be supported using the MapInfo functionality for superimposing maps on the aerial photographs \citep{Chapman} or data formatting in resilience studies \citep{HERMIDA2017467}.

However, all these proprietary programs are restricted to the licence availability for usage. Such software products work well in business enterprises and commercial organisations with available market license. This becomes increasingly inefficient for many research cases, such as educational workspace, private-run business or mapping for personal needs. In such cases, using open source program for processing geographic data is required for effective workflow. Existing examples of mapping Panama using open source available are presented by QGIS \citep{VARDEMAN2020e01098,Bennett,YAMADA201642} which is a most inventive open source GIS competitive to the ArcGIS in functionality and availability of plugins. Advanced software GRASS GIS achieves attention in environmental mapping owing to the variety of function that can be applied in geological and topographic mapping \citep{Lemenkova202125}. Using ILWIS GIS is often applied for satellite image processing and Diva GIS may be used for vegetation mapping. The examples of using SAGA GIS for mapping Panama include forest dynamics with time series of land cover types \citep{WALKER2020102029}, sensitivity analyses of performance parameters in energy studies \citep{VERA20201399}, or LiDAR-based spatial analysis of tropical landscapes \citep{GRAVES201832}.

Nevertheless, the available GIS programs, briefly reviewed above, have significant limitations and may be efficient for certain operations only, while restricted for automatic and complex spatial data analysis and visualization. This is mainly because these programs are designed for special needs of geographical data analysis, but perform poorly or none when programming scripts are required for large data processing. Another case of concern is regarded to the origin of data and materials, which may be captured from multiple sources, have diverse projections and resolution. The combination of these tools in one research for dividing research tasks is cumbersome and not always convenient, which raises up the question of the console-based program for spatial data analysis and mapping, which has a more flexibility in mapping process. This is achieved in Generic Mapping Tools (GMT), owing to the embedded syntax of the programming language, which is used for preparing maps in this study, as demonstrated below.

\section{Study Area}

Panama is an important region of Central America and a transatlantic key transport hub. Therefore, it presents interest both for economists and geographers (Figure \ref{fig:01}).

The Panama Canal plays a key economic role, connecting transshipment hub of the world maritime trade. It has one of the busiest traffic density in Latin America \cite{Rodrigue,RodrigueAshar}. However, the region is located in active tectonic area with high seismicity which affects topographic and environmental setting. The anthropogenic effects include intensive industrial activities which results in river damming, forest flooding, species invasion, salt intrusions, chemical pollution \cite{Salgado} and deforestation \cite{Oestreicher}. Regional tectonic and geological setting result in active construction works, hydroelectric potential, mineral resource exploration \cite{Palacio,Corral}. 

The Panama Isthmus is notable for the geological complexity where volcanic fronts of eastern Central America are interrupted by a topographic low of tectonic and magmatic origin \cite{Buchs}. It explains the deep links between the seismicity, volcanism and topography in Panama owing to active tectonic faulting, which impeded the development of high topography in the area of canal. Deep associations between the geophysical and topographic setting and environmental sustainability of Panama are scarcely explained in the existing literature, except for selected studies. For instance, strong control of geology and topography on soil phosphorus in tropical rainforests of Panama results in biochemical changes that have consequences for the biodiversity and distribution of plant species in ecosystems of Panama \cite{Dieter}. 

Tectonic plates subduction is a major cause of earthquakes and high seismicity along the Pacific margins \cite{Lemenkova202032,Lemenkova2019107}. Panama forms a part of the tectonic microplate resting on the Caribbean and Nazca plates that subduct under the Cocos and South America plates \cite{Demets,Bergoeing}. Such active tectonic processes result in a series of repetitive earthquakes, notable at various depths, as reported by the IRIS seismic center. The links between the tectonic activity, submarine volcanism and seafloor geomorphology in the Panama Basin are detected by faulted volcanic ridges that are created by the voluminous eruptions at the Galapagos hot spots. The geomorphic elements also take place in this area: grabens, active deep-sea erosion and submarine slopes \cite{Lonsdale}. 

The tectonic subduction of the Panama Fracture Zone is an evolutionary process that creates variations in convergence rate, obliquity, and crustal thickness at its intersection with the Middle America Trench. This process is maintained by the southeast migrating of the Panama Triple Junction (Cocos-Nazca-Caribbean). Another factor of high seismicity is the development of the inner forearc thrust belt in the colliding Cocos Ridge and Nazca–Caribbean convergence, east of the triple junction. This results in the thrust faults in the Panama Fracture Zone and supports the high seismicity in the region \cite{Morell}. Further details on the tectonic and geologic setting of Panama are described earlier \cite{Wolters,Vergara,Cardona,Fuchs,Hardy}. 

The interplay between the geochemical and geologic setting and terrestrial and ocean ecosystems is illustrated by \cite{Gardner} who highlighted the links between the amount of rock-derived Molybdenum and water sources in Panama (precipitation, canopy, soil, groundwater, seeps, streams). Other studies shown that geochemical trends are associated with multiple factors, e.g., soil, topography and land use \cite{Neumann}. The processes in seasonal tropics are highly complicated due to the frequent changes and regional specifics, e.g., soil decay, insect activity, weathering \cite{Niedzialek}. Linking the multi-source data is essential for interpreting landscape determinants, risk assessment and providing new information on environmental functionality and structure \cite{Higgins,Lemenkova202112,Klauco2017}. Various approaches to spatial data analysis can be used for effective environmental mapping including comparative data analysis.

At the same time, Panama is an environmentally vulnerable region. It includes precious mangrove estuaries exposed to the environmental effects from land and sea, with the high degree of connectivity between the coastal and terrestrial ecosystems \cite{Valiela}. Forested mountains in western Panama (Figure \ref{fig:02}) provide sources of water, materials for agriculture, and industry, and hydroelectric energy \cite{Larsen}. Mangroves are one of the most productive ecosystems in the world, providing existence for coastal livelihoods \cite{LopezAngarita}. Marine protected areas of Panama include rare species, benthic and fish communities and resources, e.g., Las Perlas Archipelago \cite{Mair}. Natural ecosystems are linked with regional topography and climate setting and experience the effects from the geophysical processes \cite{Benfield,Lee,Lemenkova202018,Ruane,Lemenkova202206,Klauco2013,Lemenkova202140}. For instance, the example of the climate effects on coastal ecosystems is given by \cite{Sadatzki} who noted phytoplankton blooms during seasonal coastal upwelling that affected the growth rate of the species. Geochemical studies in Panama identified earlier the contribution of dissolved organic carbon (DOC) to the oceans from the mountainous rivers, which illustrates the influence of lithology and topography on the environment. The correlations between land cover, bedrock lithology and marine sedimentation in the shelf show associations \cite{Goldsmith}.

\section{Methodology}

GIS are standard tools for cartographic visualization in a variety of applications in environmental sciences, e.g., forest conservation and deforestation, mapping landscapes and protected zones, marine mapping, geologic research, etc. \cite{Harper,Pelletier,Lemenkova202135,Rucker}. However, the limitations of mapping in the traditional GIS are essential, due to the lack of automation in data processing. Besides, an open problem in the existing commercial GIS is data format handling. In contrast, scripting methods of cartographic approach present more in-depth ways of data processing and visualization \cite{1405673,Lemenkova202213}. Scripting ensures effective solution to automation in data processing. It can efficiently and rapidly manipulate with a wide range of geographic datasets and materials, including both vector and bitmap data. In this study, we present the application of GMT, which is an effective cartographic tool. It a significant advantage over the conventional GIS as a machine-based mapping method in cartography. This is largely explained by its scripting functionality that differs significantly from the traditional GIS. 

\subsection{Data}
The following data were used for mapping. The IRIS seismological data from 1740 records (http://ds.iris.edu/ds/nodes/dmc/data/) have been collected as CSV table covering 40 years (1970-2021). These seismic events are located around the Panama coastline and vary according to their magnitude, depth of earthquake focus, precise date and time of event and coordinate location. The topographic data used as a basis map for Figure \ref{fig:01} and Figure \ref{fig:04} are from the GEBCO \cite{GEBCO,Schenke2016} and ETOPO1 grid \cite{Amante} used for 3D model in Figure \ref{fig:02}. The geoid data were downloaded from EGM-2008 \cite{Pavlis}. Gravity anomalies (Figures \ref{fig:05} and \ref{fig:06}) were taken from the remote sensing based observations available from Scripps Institution of Oceanography based on the CryoSat-2 and Jason-1 satellite observations \cite{Sandwell}. These data were imported into the GMT environment for processing. Apart from these layers, we used the built-in data of world coastlines, riverine networks, state boundaries and polygons of countries which available from the integration of GMT with DCW.

\subsection{Generic Mapping Tools} 

\subsubsection{Scripting approach}

The scripting approach of GMT builds upon command line arguments required by each module of GMT. By changing the options of commands in GMT, mapping can be performed semi-automatically for similar layouts, which includes the repeatability of scripts. Thus, in this study, the GMT was used for 2D and 3D mapping \cite{Wessel} using scripts for plotted six maps available in the author's GitHub public repository with open access: \href{https://github.com/paulinelemenkova/Mapping_Panama_GMT_Scripts}{https://github.com/paulinelemenkova/Mapping\_Panama\_GMT\_Scripts}. The scripts were written using the XCode and then run from the console of the GMT. The metadata of the spatial datasets were carefully checked and then data were visualized by GMT in the Mercator projection for comparability reasons. 

\subsubsection{Data processing}

Apart from basic geographic data manipulation, we used available GMT raster data processing tools , including subsetting and raster gridding. The cartographic projecting was selected from the available 31 projections, adjustable for the study region of Panama, using the -J flag. For example, to examine the elevation component in the topographic records, this study used the GDAL-based option in the 'gdalinfo' utility. To visualize raster grids and to add contour lines (isolines), this study applied the following modules of the GMT: ‘gmt grdimage pa\_relief.nc -Cpauline.cpt -R276.5/283/6.5/10.5  -JM6.0i -P -I+a15+ne0.75 -t30 -Xc -K > \$p’, and the ‘gmt grdcontour pa\_relief1.nc -R -J -C200 -W0.1p -O -K >> \$ps’. 

The isolines of the geoid curvature are notably curved. according to the topographic elevations, as can be noted in Figure \ref{fig:03}. For the case ofgeoid, the isolines were plotted every 0.5 m. It is shown that the isolines of the gravity anomalies and gradient gravity grid curvatures (Figures \ref{fig:05} and \ref{fig:06}) form a set of curved lines on the maps, which well correspond to the topographic elevations and oceanic depression in the margins of the Gulf of Panama and the continental slope of the Caribbean Sea.  

Subsetting data from the raw dataset is particularly useful in case of big data (millions of cells, Gb of size), where spatial extent is well known beforehand. For this procedure, this study used the GMT module: 'gmt grdcut GEBCO\_2019.nc -R276.5/283/6.5/10.5  -Gpa\_relief.nc'. Here the ‘-R’ flag signifies that the study extent in the west-east-south-north (WESN) convention: ‘-R276.5/283/6.5/10.5’ (0 to 360° from W to E, 0 to 90° from S to N). 

\subsubsection{Data visualization}
A highly adjustable tools of GMT enable to perform print-quality maps and graphical illustrations through a large variety of color palette tables (CPTs), lines and polygons. Thus, the legends of color palette scales on the maps determine possible range interval for the data extent, within which the variations of the data extension can be observed (Figure \ref{fig:01} to \ref{fig:06}), and define the concentration and distribution of the extreme values of the geoid undulations, geophysical grids and seismic magnitude with respect to the topography of the country. The example of the legend (here for the topographic map in Figure \ref{fig:01}) in GMT script is as follows: ‘gmt psscale -Dg276.5/6.05+w15.3c/0.4c+h+o0.0/0i+ml -R -J -Cpauline.cpt -Bg500a500f50+l"Color scale 'wiki-celtic-sea': ... [R=-3954/2426, discrete, RGB, 25 segments]" -I0.2 -By+lm -O -K >> \$ps’. 

\begin{figure}[H]
\begin{center}
	\includegraphics[width=0.99\linewidth]{figures/fig_02.jpg}

\caption{3D data modelling using GMT scripting: topographic relief of Panama and coastal bathymetry. Map source: author.}

One can note here that the lines of code can be broken into a series of commands with options. Thus, the most important line of code have the following meaning. The ‘-Bg500a500f50’ flag controls the frequency, annotation, tick, and gridline interval for the color legend; the ‘-Dg276.5/6.05’ defines the reference start point on the map for the color scale using the the ‘-Dg’ for map coordinates (in this cases: Mercator projections); the ‘-I0.2’ flag adds the illumination effects; the ‘-Cpauline.cpt ’ defines the selected color palette, which was pre-defined using the previous command: ‘gmt makecpt -Cwiki-celtic-sea.cpt > pauline.cpt’.

\subsubsection{Data modelling}
Modelling complex topographic surfaces in enabled by GMT through 3D perspective mesh-models, plots and geographic maps. This is useful to illustrate the shapes of the terrain for detailed geomorphological and bathymetric analysis. Therefore, the advanced data processing step included 3D modelling using GMT-style syntax. The procedure of clipping the data to insert a small global map in Figure \ref{fig:01} was used by 'psclip' module (gmt psclip -R276.5/283/6.5/10.5  -JM6.0i pa.txt -O -K >> \$ps) to visulized the location of Panama within the global context by the Digital Chart of the World (DCW), and to highlight the study area overlaid upon the semi-translucent image of the neighboring countries. For plotting coastal lines and borders of the countries, the 'pscoast' module was used as follows: 'gmt pscoast -R -J -Ia/thinner,blue -Na -N1/thicker,darkred -W0.1p -Df -O -K >> \$ps'. 

\subsubsection{Spatial analysis}
To account for complex variables (free-air gravity, geodetic data), we present an integration method of plotting data in identical cartographic projection, for comparability, and to improve the efficiency of the visualization of data with different resolution and sources. Specifically, by representing geographical and geophysical  phenomena on each maps from the series, we derive information regarding the level of seismicity in the surrounding areas, to visualise the most vulnerable areas. We show though the comparison of presented maps that the GMT script-based method performed well for generating a series of the thematic mapping covering coastal regions, such as Panama.

\begin{figure}[H]
\begin{center}
	\includegraphics[width=0.99\linewidth]{figures/fig_03.jpg} 
\end{center}
\caption{Geoid undulations visualised using GMT console scripting. Map source: author.}

To investigate spatial variations of the earthquake events, the ‘psxy’ module of GMT was used for visualization of the events. Technically, the procedure was performed by reading the (x,y,z) triplets derived from the file in .ngdc format and visualized by the code that plots symbols at the coordinate locations, as follows: ‘gmt psxy -R -J quakes\_PA\_s.ngdc -Wfaint -i4,3,6,6s0.05 -h3 -Scc -Csteps.cpt -O -K >> \$ps’. The earthquake events were plotted using the '-Sc' flag that visualises circles with the size given as the diameter of the circle signifying the magnitude of the earthquake. The specification of the code was applied from the GMT manual, and the IRIS file was visualized as the converted table (.ngdc), to determine all the earthquakes. The magnitude and spatial variability of the earthquakes was indicated in the ‘-i4,3,6,6s0.05’ flag that signifies the numbers of columns in the table with the following names, respectively: Year, Month, Day, Lat, Lon, Depth, Mag. 

\subsubsection{Advantages and robustness}
As demonstrated above, the algorithm approach of scripts by GMT is largely based on using its syntax, which is very similar to the programming languages \cite{Lemenkov202103,Lemenkov202107}. The lines of codes perform operations controlling the appearance of the cartographic elements on the maps and form a script  six maps plotted in PS format converted to JPG and presented in Figures \ref{fig:01} to \ref{fig:06}. Apart from the traditional JPG and GMT standard netCDF format, it recognises a variety of miscellaneous grid formats, such as SUN, GEODAS, ASCII, AGC, Surfer and data capture via GDAL. Using GMT for mapping lead to a higher quality of the produced maps, and lower time required for workflow due to the reduced time required for scripting, compared to the cumbersome and time-consuming data processing in GIS. Thus, in contrast to GIS, GMT presents a free set of command-line tools and options for processing of geographic data in both raster and vector formats.

\section{Results}

Different types of maps containing the visualization of complex geophysical parameters of Panama, 2D and 3D modelling, the effects from the geoid on elevation heights and seismicity were rendered  using the GMT modules. The color distribution palettes were adjusted to each of the maps and implemented using embedded CPTs, so the colors were produced and selected to best indicate the variability in the studied parameters. 

\begin{figure}[H]
\begin{center}

\end{center}
\caption{Seismicity in Panama based on the IRIS database (1970-2021). Map source: author.}

We compared the topographic map and the geoid model and found that the exceptionally high values of geoid (Figure \ref{fig:03}) can be seen in the high mountainous regions of the Panama (values 17–20 m in the region of Cordilliera de Talamanca, southern coast of Peninsula de Azuero and easter regions of Panama, 77.5°-78.5°W). This well corresponds to the topographic roughness in the highlands. In contrast, negative values of geoid can be seen over the Caribbean Sea basin in the north (from -4 to 0 m). High resolution mapping of the seismicity (Figure \ref{fig:04}) revealed 1740 individual seismic events in Panama located at various depths. The earthquakes had different magnitudes and spatial location. A notable concentration of the earthquakes is seen in the western region of the Panama's shelf waters (~82°-83.5W), and on the border with Colombia (~77-78.5°W). The earthquakes vary by magnitudes from 2.9 to 7.8 at the depths from 0 (shallow earthquakes) to 225 m (near the west coast of Colombia), that are traceable from the IRIS seismicity website and visualised in this study using the GMT (Figure \ref{fig:04}).

The hallmark of the high values in free-air gravity anomalies (Figure \ref{fig:05} and \ref{fig:06}) and vertical gradient is a concentration of the land masses in the mountain areas of Panama. Specifically, the higher geophysical anomalies can be noted by the comparison of Figure \ref{fig:05} with \ref{fig:06}, and Figures \ref{fig:01} with \ref{fig:02}. the comparison is made using the maps prepared by the GMT codes and manually setting the parameters to produce the highest possible image quality, while matching the projection and color palette tables. Thus, the lowest values correlate with the bathymetric depression of the seafloor along the western coasts of Panama which continues the extent Middle America Trench in the north \cite{Lemenkova2019108}. The topographic and geophysical patterns are compared to the seismicity with detected correlation in the zone of the tectonic subduction and earthquakes. This supports previous studies regarding the associations between the seismicity and topographic roughness of the seafloor \cite{Bilek}.

The appearance of the high values in gravity anomalies (over 220 mGal) in the mountainous regions matches the geodynamic processes associated with the Earth structure, inner interior, and crustal surface, as well as the geodetic and geophysical effects \cite{Lemenkova202104}. This regards the shape and rotation of the Earth and land mass distribution, which results in the contrasting topographic patterns and distribution of the topographic elevations: depressions, deep oceanic trenches and  mountain ranges. The results correspond to the existing reports on the seismic activity and tectonic instability in the Panama region. Specifically, the presented maps of seismically active areas around Panama correspond to prior studies that indicated that Costa Rica Ridge and the Panama Fracture Zone are significant tectonic features related to the thermal anomalies in Panama \cite{Vargas}. The recordings of earthquakes on the oceanic bottom seismographs shown magmatic and hydrothermal activity of the upper lithosphere in Panama region that are a source of seismic hazards and risks. Using the comparison of gravity, geoid and earthquake data, the relationship between the seismic activity and topography of Panama was explored. These maps are applicable as indicator and inventory data sources to analyse hazard vulnerability and perform risk assessment in the regions prone to seismic activity along the coasts of both the Caribbean Sea and the Pacific Ocean.

The results indicate that the most notable series of earthquakes seismic activity are concentrated in the following regions of Panama: 

\begin{enumerate}
	\item south-western region (square of 82°-83°W, 7°-10°N), owing to the tectonic faulting; 
	\item south-eastern region in the border with Colombia (square 77°-78.8°W, 6°-8.5°N) due to the tectonically active margins of the subducting Nazca Plate along the South American continent; 
	\item sporadic, less prominent earthquake activity in the southern coasts of he Caribbean Sea (78°-80°W, 9.3°-10°N), which notes the movements of the Caribbean plate colliding with the South American and Cocos plates in a more complex tectonic movement. 
\end{enumerate}	

Earthquakes, their spatial distribution, magnitude and frequency, are an important source indicating hazard risks in the region. At the same time, earthquakes are significantly influenced by a variety of regional geologic and tectonic factors. These include, among others, lithosphere plate subduction, run-off, topography, distribution of geologic fault lines and regional geologic context, soil type, (clayey, sandy, etc.). In this context, highlighting the correlations between these parameters enables to take a deeper insight into the factors controlling the seismicity in Central America. Thus, we can note that the distribution of continuous fields on the gravity grids is correlated with topography, as reflected in a correspondence with isolines. At the same time, the gravity is poorly correlated with seismicity and active tectonic zones in western Panama, 81°-82.5°W), although there is a certain impact of the geodynamic phenomena on geological processes. 

The tectonics of the Caribbean Sea and the marginal eastern Pacific is known to largely influence regional geologic variability and seismicity. Another factor is the conjunction with a greater topographic heterogeneity in both the terrestrial areas and seafloor bathymetry \cite{Duncan,Idarraga,Naranjo,RodriguezZurrunero}. This deep interplay between the geophysics, geology and tectonics explains the general correlation between the seismicity and topography as well as the geology and geophysics \cite{Lemenkova202018}. At the same time, when this correlation is analysed within regional zones of Panama (SW, northern coasts of the Caribbean Sea, SE at the Colombia affected by the Andean geology), one can note a stronger correlations that are observed between the magnitude, distribution and frequency of earthquakes.

\section{Discussion}
We have presented an advanced method for the automatic plotting of a series of maps showing geophysical setting of Panama made using scripts written on GMT syntax. The functionality of GMT for mapping geophysical and topographic visualisation of Panama demonstrated effective results in terms of mapping quality and cartographic workflow. The maps illustrate a high level of seismicity in the Gulf of Panama, caused by active tectonic movements, which result from the colliding lithosphere plates. High seismicity in this region presents risks both for the economic functionality of the Gulf of Panama, which is a strategic maritime port connecting the Atlantic and Pacific oceans, and for the environmental sustainability. We have demonstrated the effectiveness of scripts and GMT methods both in terms of illustrative quality of the presented cartographic materials and technical soundness of the programming approach for geosciences. To the best of our knowledge, no existing maps made using the same datasets and identical extent by the GMT scripts, exist in the available literature.

The Gulf of Panama is a strategic maritime hub of high logistic importance for transport, which connects the Atlantic and the Pacific oceans. Therefore, mapping seismicity has a high actuality in Panam. Mitigating possible risks and predicting earthquake occurrence should always start wit evaluating the available data and maps. The earthquakes are recognised as hazardous geologic events accompanied by devastating events: landslides, tsunami, rockfalls, damage of buildings and losses of human lives. Mapping earthquakes provides an important source of information for risk assessment in the regions of high seismicity. However, such studies for Panama are limited, compared to the other Central American countries in the Andes with a long-term tradition of geological investigations.

As a response to these needs, this paper presented a series of thematic geophysical and seismic maps of Panama plotted using the practical workflow of the advanced methods of GMT programming scripts. Our main contribution is a novel series of maps covering Panama and the analysis of the variations in parameters and phenomena demonstrated on these maps. The existing associations between the geological-geophysical parameter and environmental processes have been noted in previous studies \citep{LANDAVERDEGONZALEZ2020105329,RIBEIROSOUZA2022101570,SOHEL2022100226}, as well as further remarks on socio-economic and physio-geographic links \citep{AVELAR200927,WRIGHTWENDEL2012272}. 

Besides the economic importance, the Gulf of Panama and coastal areas are notable for vulnerable environment with high biodiversity. This includes mangroves ecosystems that requires protection. Preventive geophysical monitoring of such region should be maintained by the high quality maps, aimed to indicate possible risks and to ensure transport safety. Regular monitoring and accurate visualisation of the seismically endangered areas of Panama may serve to the prognosis of earthquake hazards, and mitigate hazards for the maritime transport, which is crucial for the global trade. 

\section{Conclusion}

This article has presented a GMT script-based method for cartographic visualization of seismic activity in the region of the Gulf of Panama. Unlike previous traditional GIS-based methods of mapping which operate in the spatial domain, we performed plotting through GMT syntax using codes which ensured the comprehensive workflow for the cartographic specification of the maps. The GIS-based methods relying on graphical user interface (GUI) tend to use the pre-defined designed tools and graphical methods of representation, while the proposed GMT-based method outputs maps which is made using individual adjustments in each lines of the programming code using expressions and syntax of GMT. We have also analysed the maps pairwise and considered the underlying reasons for seismicity with regard to the topographic and geophysical setting of the region.

Mapping the marine regions benefits from using cartographic scripts due to the increased speed of plotting and automated workflow that ensures the quality of maps. Therefore, this paper applies the GMT, an advanced cartographic tool, for mapping the region of Panama based on high-resolution data including IRIS seismographic database (1970-2021). The presented methodology of this study included the examples of the explained and commented GMT scripts made using console-based techniques. The link to the GitHub repository of the author with open scripts is provided for the repeatability of the scripts. The specific focus of this article includes the seismic and geophysical mapping using the datasets on gravity and earthquakes. A series of presented maps enables the analysis of correlation between the earthquake distribution, seismicity, topographic and geophysical setting aimed at hazard mitigation and risk assessment in Panama.
