  \begin{abstract}
{This study presents a cartographic framework for exploring geophysical setting of Panama using the Generic Mapping Tools (GMT). Besides the economic importance as a maritime transport hub, the region of Panama is notable for vulnerable environment and high biodiversity. At the same time, seismic activity in this region is high due to the specific geologic setting, including tectonic plate subduction. To investigate the geophysical setting in this region through advanced mapping, we propose a GMT-based solutions for spatial data processing, which extends the classic GIS methods to the programming approach. First, gravity, topographic, and geodetic data were processed and imported into the GMT. Second, the seismicity dataset was collected from the IRIS open source (coverage 1970-2021). Third, the series of compatible maps was generated using console scripts written in GMT language. Afterwards, the variations in geophysical parameters were explored and compared on the maps to highlight the correlations between the processes. To help users reuse the presented scripts we presented the scripts in open repository for repeatability in similar projects regarding the seismicity and hazard risk assessment.}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
This article introduces a series of new maps of Panama prepared using scripting practical approach of Generic Mapping Tools (GMT). The aim is to visualise high-resolution geophysical, topographic and seismic data for analysis of their correlations. The main technical advantage of scripts consists in the repeatability. Thus, such maps can be easily fabricated for other regions of Latin America using GMT and similar datasets, and then assembled through a sequence of GMT codes that regulate the appearance of maps through modules and operations. Compared to previous approaches of using GMT \citep{McNamara,Kobayashi,Cleveland} we presented the scripts in full availability for the reuse in future studies. As some of the recent papers have shown \citep{4814952,Lewis,Zhangetal2017,Eakin} GMT is an excellent instrument for semi-automatic mapping of the geophysical variables.

\subsection{Background}
Novel cartographic technologies have been rapidly developed over the last decades to support spatial data analysis in marine and Earth sciences. Mapping exploits a wide variety of of theoretical principles and practical techniques to create real-world tangible reproductions of Earth models. A special application of mapping techniques including cartographic scripts used for geographical data processing of Panama. 

As a response to the needs for high-quality mapping of the regions in seismic risk, such as Central America, the presented approach provides an open source solution of GMT-based standardised cartographic techniques employed for mapping seismicity, topography and geophysical grids. Although the proposed scripting approach requires, to some extent, advanced skills in geospatial programming, GMT can be considered as an alternative method to GIS in terms of mapping and cartographic visualization. Thus, this approach could be beneficial in spatial analysis of geophysical setting over Central American region, such as seismicity and hazard risk assessment, where visualization of high-resolution spatial data is a key part of research.

From the point of view of geospatial applications, using GMT provides a novel formalism in spatial data processing to design a series of maps with identical parameters for comparability and compatibility. This coding formalism provides enough degree of freedom to map geophysical and seismic maps for hazard analysis in Panama -- a key transport region of Central America. Consequently, it enables to efficiently model 2D and 3D datasets that characterises the topography of the Earth. The systematic reuse of scripts has been applied from modular techniques of GMT and adjusted to the particular datasets within the framework of this project. Additionally, this study applies standard GMT coding, to achieve unification of maps through the development of scripts, integration of data and implementation of GMT modules using its standard syntax in a cartographic practice.

\subsection{Motivation}
Probably the most well-know tools for cartographic visualization and spatial analysis is Geographic Information System (GIS) \citep{Longley,Harvey}. While initially the range of GIS was very limited with restricted functionality, modern technologies enable a wide range of techniques to be used. Currently, besides creating maps, GIS enable to manipulate, organise, and display spatial information \citep{LAMONT1997469}. The cartographic tasks include terrain analysis, topology, generalisation, spatial analysis, data formatting, projecting, gridding, spatial clipping, overlay, logical queries,  extracting data, raster to vector conversion, map design, and many more. 

At the same time, the algorithms of mapping have been improved substantially along with the increase and precision of available open spatial data. Consequently, mapping accuracy in terms of reproduction has reached high cartographic standards. Nevertheless, using commercial GIS is perceived as being too expensive for the standard users, while open source GIS may have restricted functionality. Moreover, the input data have to satisfy certain geospatial characteristics (data formats, resolution, coverage and compatibility), in order to produce reliable high-quality maps representing the models of the geospatial phenomena and processes on the Earth.

Decades ago, principally new paradigms for cartographic data processing and mapping were proposed through scripting \citep{WesselSmith1998,Neteler2004,Neteler2008}. The main idea of using scripts in cartography was to drastically automate, and thus, to simplify, the overall mapping procedure by fabricating programming scripts for plotting maps, which would enable repeatability and precision of mapping. This class of methods os principally different from GIS since it relies on a simple concept: every step in a mapping process is fully controlled by the lines of code, written according to the syntax of embedded language. In such a way, hidden obstacles and bugs, often in traditional GIS, are overcome and avoided, and the complete procedure of mapping is radically improved.

\subsection{Contribution}
In this study, the GMT methods were applied and redesigned for mapping Panama in order to demonstrate the efficient digital series of maps on geophysical, topographic and seismic parameters of the territory of the country. Thus, this study proposes an updated strategy of using scripts to plot illustrative visualisations of topographic and geophysical setting of the country using high-resolution recent data. Furthermore, this paper extends the classical GIS workflow of mapping by integrating programming methods of data processing in geographical studies. In addition, the designed of map in the presented series was formulated using GMT scripts, to achieve the high map printing quality in a novel, structurally sound perspective of cartographic workflow. We have demonstrated how GMT syntax can be exploited to efficiently represent complex geophysical models of the terrain. 

Thus, using GMT, we proposed an efficient strategy of mapping to visualise several geophysical variables using different datasets in a united series of maps. In addition, the GMT mapping method also takes into account both 2D and 3D data visualization, which has been demonstrated in this article. the presented scripting method can be used for analysis of correlations between the data for showing various continuous fields on compatible maps to take advantage of data comparison for a better perception of links and association among the geophysical and topographic parameters. In such a way, this articles presents an automated procedure of mapping to ensure that quality of maps.

\section{Related Works}
Accurate cartographic visualization has been a long-standing challenge in marine science and oceanic engineering, particularly when the data and materials vary according to different data sources, resolution, and spatial extent. The wide range of geographic datasets poses a great challenge for mapping approaches due to their different functionality: desktop GIS, remote sensing GIS, server GIS and so on \citep{STEINIGER2013136}. Therefore, selecting suitable GIS program is important prior to mapping, since the functionality of program may affect the spatial analysis and limit cartographic techniques. A complete review of various GIS is beyond the scope of this article. We refer the read to the for a comprehensive survey on available free GIS \citep{LEIDIG201549,CHEN2010253}, recent trends in the development of cartography \citep{WangFu} and new directions and perspectives in GIS \citep{Lietal2022,DUBE2022661}, while this section only covers the most relevant GIS applications for environmental mapping.

Many existing algorithms of data processing are available only in certain programs and software. For example, some well-know commercial Geographic Information System (GIS) are focused on bitmap or LiDAR data processing. The efficient solutions are presented by ERDAS Imagine with multiple applications of image processing of Panama \citep{Guzman,SANCHEZAZOFEIFA20092076,PORTILLOQUINTERO2010144}
The Global Mapper GIS, includes a variety of tools including LiDAR handling, 3D rendering, watershed delineation. It is developed by the USGS U.S. survey, a provider of geological and environmental data, used in studies of Panama \citep{WALKER2021105632,HOPP2016187,ODEA201259}. 

Probably the most-well known and widely distributed commercial GIS is the ArcGIS which perform well both for vector and raster data including LiDAR \citep{HIGGINS2014358}, Landsat TM images processing \citep{SESNIE20082145}, deforestation analysis of mangroves ecosystems \citep{LOPEZANGARITA201882,MEJIARENTERIA2018233}, analysis of traffic in the Panama Canal and conservation \citep{GUZMAN2020104113,DARIEN2021105490,ROMPRE2008129} and hydrological basin analysis, due to the extensive spatial modelling tools available in the ArcGIS \citep{SALGADO2020138444,PEREZCONSUEGRA2021107590}. The Geomedia GIS software, developed by the Hexagon Geospatial, was a concurrent for the ArcGIS for a long period, mostly used for cadastral mapping, landscape and terrain analysis and spatial modelling for geomorphometry tasks \citep{KLINGSEISEN2008109}. The analysis of tropical environment, marine reserves and coral reef can be supported using the MapInfo functionality for superimposing maps on the aerial photographs \citep{Chapman} or data formatting in resilience studies \citep{HERMIDA2017467}.

However, all these proprietary programs are restricted to the licence availability for usage. Such software products work well in business enterprises and commercial organisations with available market license. This becomes increasingly inefficient for many research cases, such as educational workspace, private-run business or mapping for personal needs. In such cases, using open source program for processing geographic data is required for effective workflow. Existing examples of mapping Panama using open source available are presented by QGIS \citep{VARDEMAN2020e01098,Bennett,YAMADA201642} which is a most inventive open source GIS competitive to the ArcGIS in functionality and availability of plugins. Advanced software GRASS GIS achieves attention in environmental mapping owing to the variety of function that can be applied in geological and topographic mapping \citep{Lemenkova202125}. Using ILWIS GIS is often applied for satellite image processing and Diva GIS may be used for vegetation mapping. The examples of using SAGA GIS for mapping Panama include forest dynamics with time series of land cover types \citep{WALKER2020102029}, sensitivity analyses of performance parameters in energy studies \citep{VERA20201399}, or LiDAR-based spatial analysis of tropical landscapes \citep{GRAVES201832}.

Nevertheless, the available GIS programs, briefly reviewed above, have significant limitations and may be efficient for certain operations only, while restricted for automatic and complex spatial data analysisc and visualization. This is mainly because these programs are designed for special needs of geographical data analysis, but perform poorly or none when programming scripts are required for large data processing. Another case of concern is regarded to the origin of data and materials, which may be captured from multiple sources, have diverse projections and resolution. The combination of these tools in one research for dividing research tasks is cumbersome and not always convenient, which raises up the question of the console-based program for spatial data analysis and mapping, which has a more flexibility in mapping process. This is achieved in Generic Mapping Tools (GMT), owing to the embedded syntax of the programming language, which is used for preparing maps in this study, as demonstrated below.

\section{Study Area}

Panama is an important region of Central America and a transatlantic key transport hub. Therefore, it presents interest both for economists and geographers (Figure \ref{fig:01}). The Panama Canal plays a key economic role, connecting transshipment hub of the world maritime trade. It has one of the busiest traffic density in Latin America \cite{Rodrigue,RodrigueAshar}. However, the region is located in active tectonic area with high seismicity which affects topographic and environmental setting. The anthropogenic effects include intensive industrial activities which results in river damming, forest flooding, species invasion, salt intrusions, chemical pollution \cite{Salgado} and deforestation \cite{Oestreicher}. Regional tectonic and geological setting result in active construction works, hydroelectric potential, mineral resource exploration \cite{Palacio,Corral}.
